What Sympathetic Sitar does

Underneath the playing strings of a real sitar lies a second set of strings the player never touches: the taraf, or sympathetic strings. They are tuned to the notes of the raga, and they ring on their own whenever the music passes over their pitch — a silvery halo that shadows every phrase.

Sympathetic Sitar gives that halo to any instrument. Send a guitar, a synth, a voice — anything — through it, and a bank of up to 48 tuned strings rings along with what you play. You choose the tuning: the strings follow the scale and key you pick, from familiar Western scales to Turkish makams and Hindustani ragas with their authentic in-between notes.

Play a note the strings know, and it blooms. Play a chord, and the whole bank shimmers back at you, slowly fading like a piano with the sustain pedal down. You are not adding an effect on top of your sound — you are playing a roomful of strings with it.

Good to know: the strings only ring at their own pitches. Put the pedal after the effects you want the strings to hear (drive, wah …) and before big ambient effects like reverb and delay, so the halo washes into the room with everything else.

Quick start

  1. Add Sympathetic Sitar to your pedalboard. In the MOD web interface, find it under the brand Stefan and drag it into your signal chain.
  2. Pick your key. Set ROOT to the key of your song and leave SCALE on Major (or pick the scale you are playing in).
  3. Press TEST. The pedal plucks each string in turn, low to high, so you can hear exactly what the halo is tuned to. Press again to stop.
  4. Play. Notes that belong to the scale make the strings bloom and ring after you.
  5. Balance it. Turn MIX up for more halo, and DECAY up for a longer ring.
The one trick to learn: match ROOT (and SCALE) to the music you are playing. Strings tuned to the wrong key will still ring — at the wrong notes. If the halo ever sounds sour, the tuning is the first thing to check.

The controls

Selectors & buttons

ControlWhat it does
1 SCALE Which notes the strings are tuned to. 24 scales: fourteen Western (major, minors, modes, pentatonics, blues …), four Turkish makams (Rast, Uşşak, Hicaz, Saba) and six Hindustani ragas (Yaman, Bhairav, Bhairavi, Todi, Marwa, Malkauns) — the makams and ragas include their authentic in-between pitches that a piano cannot play — plus any you build yourself (see Make your own scale).
2 ROOT The key the scale is built on: C through B. Match it to your song.
3 STEREO How the strings spread across the stereo image. Mono keeps them centred; Linear lays them low-to-left, high-to-right; Wide alternates strings far left and far right. The Narrow variants are the same layouts at half width.
4 TEST Plucks each active string in turn, low to high, a couple of seconds each — hear the whole tuning without playing a note. Stops by itself at the last string, or press again to stop early.

Knobs

5 GATE

Keeps hiss and hum from ringing the strings between phrases.

  • Zero: everything excites the strings, noise included
  • Low (default): tames a quiet noise floor
  • High: only confident playing gets through — ringing tails are never cut off, only new excitement

6 SENS

How easily the strings ring.

  • Low: resonance builds slowly — a faint, distant halo
  • Max (default): strings answer your full playing strength

7 STRINGS

How many strings ring, counted from the root upward (1–48).

  • Few: a focused chord of resonance
  • 13 (default): almost two octaves of scale
  • Many: a dense four-octave curtain

8 OCT

The octave the string bank starts in (2–6).

  • 2: deep drone territory
  • 3 (default): sits with guitar and voice
  • 5–6: strings float above your notes and only their harmonics ring — pure sparkle

9 DECAY

How long the strings ring on after you stop.

  • Low: a dry, percussive pluck
  • Noon (default): a graceful one-breath fade
  • High: minutes-long, nearly a drone

10 BLOOM

Lets ringing strings excite each other through a shared bridge, the way a real sitar's tarafs do.

  • Off (default): each string rings alone
  • High: tails swell and blossom with extra shimmer

11 JAWARI

The buzzing bridge of a real sitar — a gentle, singing grit on the ringing strings. Adds character, not loudness.

  • Off (default): clean, bell-like ringing
  • High: full sitar buzz, tone-tamed so it never turns brittle

12 MIX

The balance between your dry sound and the strings. Loudness stays even across the whole sweep.

  • Left: dry only
  • Right: strings only — play the resonance itself

13 LEVEL

Output volume trim, ±12 dB, after the mix.

  • Use it to match the pedal's on/off loudness by ear

Make your own scale

The 24 built-in scales cover a lot of ground — but you can also define your own, with any intervals you like, including the microtonal pitches between the piano keys. Custom scales are saved with your pedalboard.

  1. Click the pencil (✎) in the pedal's top-right corner to open the User Scales editor.
  2. Pick a slot (User 1–8) and type a name.
  3. Enter the intervals separated by commas. Each is either a ratio such as 9/8 or 3/2, or a value in cents — a number with a decimal point, such as 347.4. The root (1/1) is added for you, so just list the notes above it.
  4. Press Save. Your scale now appears in the SCALE menu under User; select it like any other scale, and press TEST to hear the tuning.
Ratios or cents? Ratios are exact just-intonation, ideal for harmonic tunings and for matching a raga or makam by ear. Cents match how most tuning tables and theory tools write intervals — you can mix both in the same scale.

To change a scale, reopen the editor, choose its slot, edit and Save again; Clear slot removes it. Custom scales live in the pedalboard — save a MOD preset to carry one between projects.

Recipes

Knob positions are given like a clock face: fully left is 7 o'clock, the middle is noon, fully right is 5 o'clock.

Classic taraf halo

SCALE Major · ROOT your key · STRINGS noon · OCT 3 · DECAY noon · BLOOM off · JAWARI 9 o'clock · MIX 10 o'clock

The real-sitar experience: every phrase you play trails a silver shadow. Fingerpicked guitar practically turns into a different instrument.

Shimmer ceiling

OCT 5 · DECAY 1 o'clock · BLOOM noon · JAWARI off · MIX noon · STEREO Wide

The strings sit far above your notes, so only their harmonics ring — an airy sparkle floating over everything, close to a shimmer reverb but tuned to your song.

Tanpura drone bed

SCALE Raga Yaman · STRINGS 9 o'clock · DECAY 3 o'clock · BLOOM 1 o'clock · JAWARI noon · MIX 2 o'clock

A few strings that barely stop ringing. Play the root and fifth a couple of times to charge them up, then improvise over the drone with your hands free.

Microtonal colour

SCALE Hicaz or Saba · ROOT to taste · DECAY noon · JAWARI 10 o'clock · MIX 11 o'clock

The makams tune strings to pitches between the piano keys. Press TEST and listen — then play simple lines and let the halo answer with intervals your frets don't have.

Subtle studio thickener

MIX 9 o'clock · DECAY 10 o'clock · GATE 2–3 · STEREO Wide Narrow · LEVEL to match

Barely-there resonance that widens and sweetens a part without announcing itself. If you can point at the effect, turn MIX down a hair more.

Tips

When in doubt, press TEST

After every scale or root change, TEST plays you the exact tuning of the halo. It is the fastest way to learn what the makams and ragas sound like, too.

Sour halo? Check the key

Strings ring at their own pitches no matter what you play. If the resonance clashes, ROOT or SCALE doesn't match the song. Chromatic scale follows anything — at the cost of focus.

Raise GATE until the silence is silent

Amp hiss and hum will happily ring the strings all day. Turn GATE up until nothing sounds when you are not playing — tails still ring out in full.

Roll your own tuning

Beyond the 24 presets, the pencil (✎) editor in the top-right corner builds custom microtonal scales from ratios or cents — see Make your own scale. They save with the pedalboard.

Bass in, mud out? Raise OCT

With a bass-heavy source, the low strings can ring into mud. OCT 4 or 5 moves the whole bank above the muck, keeping just the sheen.

More strings, same loudness

The pedal balances its own volume as STRINGS changes — many strings each ring slightly softer, so sweeping the knob never blows up the mix.

Installing Sympathetic Sitar

Download the build for your platform from the GitHub Releases page — each release ships a MOD Desktop bundle (sitar-vX.Y.Z-linux-x86_64.tar.gz) and a MOD Dwarf bundle (sitar-vX.Y.Z-dwarf-aarch64.tar.gz).

MOD Desktop (Linux)

  1. Extract the archive: tar xzf sitar-vX.Y.Z-linux-x86_64.tar.gz
  2. Move the extracted sitar.lv2 folder into ~/Documents/MOD Desktop/lv2/
  3. Restart MOD Desktop. The pedal appears in the plugin library under the brand Stefan as Sympathetic Sitar.

MOD Dwarf

  1. Connect the Dwarf over USB and extract the Dwarf archive on your computer.
  2. Copy the bundle onto the unit: 
    scp -O -r sitar.lv2 root@192.168.51.1:/root/.lv2/
  3. Restart the audio services (or simply reboot the Dwarf): 
    ssh root@192.168.51.1 'systemctl restart jack2 mod-ui'
  4. Hard-refresh the MOD web interface in your browser (Ctrl-Shift-R) and drag the pedal onto a pedalboard.

Building from source (any LV2 host, macOS/other platforms) is covered in the project README.

Questions & answers

The strings ring notes that clash with my song.
The halo is tuned to SCALE + ROOT, not to what you play. Set ROOT to your song's key and pick the scale you are actually playing in — or choose Chromatic, which contains every note.
I hear hardly any resonance.
Check that MIX is up and SENS is high. If GATE is set high, quiet playing may not be getting through to the strings. And if OCT is 5–6 with a low-pitched instrument, only faint harmonics reach the strings — bring OCT down.
The ringing never seems to stop.
That is DECAY in its drone range, and BLOOM extends tails further still. Back DECAY down toward noon for song-friendly fades.
Can I make my own scale?
Yes — click the pencil (✎) in the pedal's top-right corner to open the User Scales editor, enter the intervals as ratios (like 3/2) or cents (like 347.4), and Save. It joins the SCALE menu under “User” and is stored with your pedalboard. See Make your own scale.
Does JAWARI make it louder?
No — it adds the buzz without changing level. If you want the halo louder, that is MIX; if you want the whole pedal louder, that is LEVEL.
